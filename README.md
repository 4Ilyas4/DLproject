During an industry-oriented project focused on spare parts identification for heavy machinery, I worked with a large, poorly structured legacy database containing technical drawings, images, and relational records. My primary responsibility was to analyze the existing data infrastructure, reconstruct implicit relationships between components, and assess the feasibility of machine learning approaches under real-world constraints.

I investigated multiple modeling strategies for mapping technical drawings to spare part identifiers. Initial experiments with supervised convolutional neural networks revealed significant challenges related to class imbalance, visual ambiguity between parts, and noise introduced during image decoding from proprietary formats. To mitigate data scarcity and improve representation quality, I explored generative models for data augmentation and trained a variational autoencoder to study latent representations of technical drawings.

Through these experiments, I observed that segmentation quality and representation robustness were the main bottlenecks rather than classifier architecture itself. This insight shifted my focus from pure classification toward representation learning and system-level considerations, such as preprocessing pipelines and data consistency. The project highlighted the limitations of end-to-end supervised learning in industrial settings and motivated my interest in more principled approaches to representation learning and model robustness
